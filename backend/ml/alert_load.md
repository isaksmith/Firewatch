# Alert load: the cry-wolf number

Generated by `backend/.venv/bin/python -m ml.alert_load` (see `alert_load.py`). Reproduce with the FIRMS archives and WFIGS file from `ml.download` and the history from `ml.build_dataset.build_history`.

## What was measured

Every satellite event first seen in March or April, 2016 to 2025, whose clustered centroid lies inside a 12 mile (19.3 km, about 450 sq mi) circle around four stand-in district centers: Comanche (37.19, -99.27), Clark (37.24, -99.82), Barber (37.23, -98.68), and Chase County, the Flint Hills worst case (38.30, -96.59). 863 events in total, no event in two circles.

Per-event features exist only in the replayed history, so each event was rebuilt from the FIRMS archives with the real clustering, `build_features(as_of=...)` at the same early-pass snapshots the training table uses (up to 3 within 24 h), the real `score_features` (xgb-v1 plus guardrails; 0 of 863 fell back to the rules scorer) and the real `confirm.decide_level`. The level shown is the highest over the snapshots (live re-leveling only raises). Monitor means nobody is texted, Investigate is the duty officer FYI text, Escalate is the "Reply PAGE" text. Weather came from Open-Meteo archive, as in training.

Events are matched to WFIGS wildfire (WF) or prescribed (RX) incidents by the training-label rule: incident within 3 km of a detection and discovered between 12 h before and 48 h after the event's life.

## Reported-fire match and false alarms

Of the 91 Investigate or Escalate events in 2023 to 2025, 1 matched a reported WF and 1 matched an RX only; 89 matched nothing (98 to 99%). **Do not read this as a 99% false-alarm rate.** WFIGS has almost no Kansas incidents near these circles: within 22 km of the three western centers in March and April there are only 3 incidents across all ten years (2 Clark, 1 Barber, 0 Comanche), and 28 near Chase, mostly RX in 2016 to 2022. An unmatched event is either a false alarm or a real fire nobody reported, and this data cannot tell which. The honest statement is: the unmatched share is an upper bound on false alarms and the registry is too sparse in the west to say anything tighter. For the reported subset, report.md's reference figures (76% of reported wildfires reach Investigate or higher, 52% Escalate) still stand.

## Training years (leakage)

2016 to 2022 are in-sample for xgb-v1, and the persistent heat-source layer uses all years. For comparison only: 27.4 texts/spring (all four) in 2016 to 2022 versus 30.3 held out, so the in-sample figure is not flattering the load, but treat 2023 to 2025 as the number. Full-period (2016 to 2025) average: 28.3 texts/spring, 14.2 per month, 11.8 Escalate/spring; worst 7 days 18.

## Limits

- Satellite only. There is no burn registry, no line-fault or neighbor-reply signal, so this probably overstates texts where a real registry would suppress registered burns (most likely in Chase). Replies and corroboration could also raise a level, which is not modeled.
- It says nothing about fires satellites miss, or about fire detected late.
- viirs-jpss2 (NOAA-21) has no public archive (404). Data covers SNPP, NOAA-20 (2018 onward) and MODIS only, so recent springs have somewhat fewer overpasses than a live system with NOAA-21 would see; the live load could be higher.
- Weather is gridded reanalysis at 0.1 degrees, hourly; live would use the app's weather source.
- Small samples: three held-out springs, and only 1 to 9 events per western district per spring. District figures are noisy; the all-four and Chase figures are steadier.
- Events are assigned by cluster centroid and by UTC first-seen month. Worst week is the highest count of Investigate plus Escalate events in any 7 consecutive days.
- No target for an acceptable text load is stated in the plan, so none is judged here.
